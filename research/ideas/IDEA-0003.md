## Observation

Published foreign-exchange research has documented cross-sectional momentum: currencies with stronger prior relative performance have, in some historical samples, subsequently outperformed weaker currencies.

The observation is treated only as a research prompt. ATS has not yet tested this family under its own data, cost, timing or universe rules.

## Proposed market context

- Market / asset class: `FX_SPOT_OTC`
- Instrument or universe: multi-currency FX universe; exact constituents not yet selected
- Horizon: multi-period momentum research; exact lookback and holding/rebalance horizons deferred to HYP-0003
- Event/session context: continuous OTC FX trading with explicit market-hour, timezone and synchronization semantics

## Preliminary rationale

A plausible mechanism is delayed information incorporation, investor under-reaction, gradual portfolio adjustment or persistent positioning across currencies.

The same literature also points to transaction costs and limits to arbitrage, so an observed gross ranking effect may not be economically executable.

This rationale is uncertain and falsifiable.

## Expected effect

- Direction/type of effect: `CROSS_SECTIONAL_CONTINUATION_OF_RELATIVE_CURRENCY_PERFORMANCE`
- Expected observable consequence: currencies ranked stronger on a predeclared past-return measure may show higher subsequent executable returns than currencies ranked weaker, under a bounded universe and explicit cost/execution conventions.

No exact universe, ranking rule, lookback, holding period, rebalance interval or target magnitude is fixed by this Idea.

## Required data

A future Hypothesis/Data Feasibility review requires:

- synchronized historical BID/ASK quotes or bars for a declared currency universe;
- explicit timestamps and market-hour/gap semantics;
- a consistent return/base-currency convention;
- point-in-time instrument availability and universe rules;
- spread and other applicable transaction-cost inputs;
- deterministic cross-sectional ranking inputs;
- a development/evaluation partition that preserves later confirmatory evidence.

The exact data provider and universe remain unselected at Idea Intake.

## Cheapest useful test

After HYP-0003 defines a bounded development policy:

1. freeze a development universe and data-quality rules;
2. evaluate every declared lookback/holding/ranking variant rather than only the best result;
3. compute executable side-aware returns with explicit cost applicability;
4. retain the complete variant denominator and sensitivity surface;
5. select or reject a fixed formulation according to a predeclared development rule;
6. only then freeze a separate confirmatory block and decision rule.

A confirmatory block that has already been revealed may not be tuned against and still be called independent evidence for the same claim.

## Known risks of false inference

- multiple testing across lookbacks, holding periods, ranking rules and universes;
- survivorship or universe-selection bias;
- inconsistent base-currency return conventions;
- asynchronous observations across currency pairs;
- transaction costs and turnover;
- stale or non-executable quotes;
- concentration in a few currencies or periods;
- dependence among overlapping holding windows;
- regime dependence;
- choosing the best published specification after reading historical literature;
- treating a gross cross-sectional spread as executable net profit.
