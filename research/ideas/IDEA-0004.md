## Preliminary rationale

A plausible mechanism is recurring variation in participation, liquidity, inventory transfer and information processing across the global FX trading day.

A visible volatility or activity pattern does not by itself imply a directional or tradable edge. Spread widening, event clustering and dependence may eliminate apparent economic value.

This rationale is uncertain and falsifiable.

## Expected effect

- Direction/type of effect: `CLOCK_SESSION_CONDITIONAL_FX_DISTRIBUTION_SHIFT`
- Expected observable consequence: one predeclared UTC/session condition may produce a reproducible change in subsequent quote-executable return or volatility distribution relative to a matching unconditional baseline.

No directional sign, session boundary, instrument, bucket width, threshold or target magnitude is fixed by this Idea.

## Cheapest useful test

After HYP-0004 freezes a bounded claim:

1. freeze one instrument/universe and a point-in-time dataset contract;
2. predeclare a small clock/session conditioning set or one fixed session condition;
3. compare the conditioned distribution with a matching unconditional baseline;
4. report spread/liquidity, sample concentration and dependence diagnostics;
5. retain every declared condition rather than only a favorable bucket;
6. apply a predeclared stop/pass rule before any later confirmatory step.

The first prototype should remain small enough to reject the line without building execution/live infrastructure.

## Known risks of false inference

- arbitrary clock-bucket selection;
- weekday, holiday or DST leakage;
- scheduled macro announcements confounding session effects;
- non-stationary market microstructure;
- spread widening at session transitions;
- multiple testing across instruments and session definitions;
- dependence among overlapping horizons;
- concentration in a few dates or regimes;
- confusing a volatility pattern with directional profitability;
- stale or non-executable quotes;
- choosing favorable session definitions after outcome inspection.
