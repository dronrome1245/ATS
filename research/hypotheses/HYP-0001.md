# Hypothesis Card — EUR/USD 1H 24-Bar Upward Breakout Continuation

## Identity

- Hypothesis ID: `HYP-0001`
- Parent Idea ID: `IDEA-0001`
- Supersedes Hypothesis ID: `null`
- Status: `READY_FOR_DATA_FEASIBILITY`
- Created on: `2026-09-21`

## Statement

For EUR/USD spot FX, when a fully completed 1H bar closes strictly above the highest BID high of the preceding 24 fully completed eligible 1H bars, the subsequent executable long return measured from the next eligible 1H bar ASK open to the BID open 24 eligible 1H bars later is expected to:

1. have a positive mean; and
2. have a greater mean than the unconditional 24-bar executable long return measured with the same ASK-entry / BID-exit convention over all eligible timestamps in the same dataset.

This is the primary claim. The `24`-bar lookback and `24`-bar forward horizon are fixed before Data Feasibility and before any prototype result is observed.

## Mechanism

The proposed mechanism is gradual price adjustment rather than instantaneous incorporation of all relevant information.

Persistent order flow, delayed reaction to macro/positioning information and cross-session position adjustment may allow a sufficiently strong upward price move to continue over subsequent hours.

The mechanism is uncertain and may be wrong. A profitable backtest is not the mechanism and is not used to define this Hypothesis.

## Market

- Market / asset class: `FX_SPOT_OTC`
- Instrument or universe: `EURUSD` / EUR/USD

## Conditions

The claim applies only when all of the following are true:

1. data are represented as ordered 1H bars with explicit BID and ASK semantics;
2. the signal bar is fully completed and available;
3. at least 24 preceding eligible 1H bars exist under the canonical session/gap policy;
4. the signal bar BID close is strictly greater than the maximum BID high of those preceding 24 bars;
5. a next eligible ASK open exists for entry modeling;
6. a BID open exists 24 eligible 1H bars after that entry point for the primary forward-return measurement;
7. no unavailable future information is used in signal formation.

The claim makes no prediction when timestamps, bar boundaries, BID/ASK alignment, session gaps or required fields cannot be represented honestly.

The Hypothesis is long-only. Downward breakouts and short positions are outside `HYP-0001`.

## Horizon

- Decision horizon: `1H`, evaluated only after the signal bar is fully closed.
- Primary effect horizon: `24 eligible 1H bars` after the modeled entry point.
- Strategy holding-period implication: `NONE` — the 24-bar horizon is the primary research measurement window, not a mandatory final strategy exit rule.

A later strategy specification may use a dynamic exit only if subsequent research justifies it. Changing the primary 24-bar research horizon after downstream use would be a material Hypothesis change.

## Expected direction

Primary expected ordering:

`mean(signal executable 24-bar return) > mean(unconditional executable 24-bar return)`

and:

`mean(signal executable 24-bar return) > 0`

Executable return means long entry at the next eligible ASK open after signal formation and exit/valuation at the BID open 24 eligible 1H bars later.

No target return magnitude is assumed.

## Decision-time information premise

At the signal timestamp, the research logic may use only:

- BID OHLC information from the signal bar and earlier completed eligible 1H bars;
- the predeclared 24-bar breakout rule;
- static metadata already known before the signal timestamp.

The research logic may not use:

- the next bar ASK open before it becomes available;
- any future BID/ASK value;
- revised future session/gap information that was unavailable at the decision time;
- a same-close fill at the signal bar close after using that close to generate the signal.

Signal formation and execution measurement are therefore separated:

`completed signal bar → signal → next eligible ASK open entry measurement`.

## Falsification

The primary claim receives evidence against it if, on the frozen feasible dataset and prespecified test:

1. the mean executable 24-bar return after qualifying breakout signals is less than or equal to zero; or
2. the mean executable 24-bar return after qualifying breakout signals is less than or equal to the unconditional executable 24-bar baseline; or
3. the apparent effect requires same-close execution, look-ahead, inconsistent BID/ASK handling or another unavailable information state; or
4. the required 24-before / 24-after observations are too sparse, dependent or corrupted to answer the claim honestly.

Economic interpretation is additionally limited if material rollover/swap costs apply to the modeled holding windows and cannot be represented. In that case, gross/quoted-spread continuation alone cannot support an economic-viability claim.

A result against the claim is retained. The lookback or horizon must not be changed in place to rescue `HYP-0001`.

## Known biases and leakage risks

- same-close execution after using the close to create the signal;
- look-ahead in rolling-high computation;
- incorrect inclusion of the signal bar inside the preceding 24-bar reference window;
- BID/ASK misalignment;
- vendor-specific FX quote differences;
- session/weekend and DST/bar-boundary differences;
- overlapping 24-bar forward windows causing dependent observations;
- repeated testing after seeing results;
- instrument-selection bias from choosing EUR/USD as the first reference instrument;
- regime dependence across volatility/monetary-policy conditions;
- ignoring rollover/swap while making multi-day economic claims;
- treating historical trend-following literature as direct evidence for this exact EUR/USD 1H claim.

## Planned cheap test

> Does the fixed 24-bar upward-breakout condition on EUR/USD 1H produce a positive mean executable 24-bar long return, and is that mean greater than the unconditional executable 24-bar baseline, when timing and BID/ASK execution are enforced?

The cheap test must:

1. use one frozen feasible dataset;
2. use the exact 24-bar breakout rule above;
3. use the exact primary 24-bar forward horizon above;
4. count and report qualifying signals;
5. report the mean signal return and unconditional baseline using the same executable-return convention;
6. report observation overlap/dependence as a limitation;
7. apply no parameter optimization after observing the result.

Secondary diagnostics may describe the distribution/path of the same prespecified observations, but they must not redefine the primary claim.
